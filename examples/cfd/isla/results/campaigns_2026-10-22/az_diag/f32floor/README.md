# F32FLOOR: the cause of ISLA's percent-level float32 floor on AeroZoo (AZ-DECIDE sub-lane, 2026-10-22)

Written 11:59 EDT, 2026-10-22. Pre-registration: PREREG.md (first version md5 966bc64e, 11:14; amended before submission
11:29, final md5 f21f92f5). Full tables: tables.md (generated by scripts/reduce_f32floor.py from out/*/*/cases.jsonl).

**Reading.** Clause 1 of the registered rule fires: float64 geometry alone removes ≥ 98.6 % of the gap on 10 of 10
high-d cases, and float64 blocks alone remove nothing (0 of 10). The trained network is not ill-conditioned in its
blocks. The rule's wording names the mechanism "float32-sensitive in raw metres, translation invariance breaks". The
secondary arms do not support that mechanism. The coordinates sit within 0.7-2.3 L of the origin, and centring them
removes the floor on only 3 of 10 cases. Two discrete steps of the geometry turn float32 roundoff into large changes:
1. **The wall-distance sign at folds.** 12-427 query points per case (of 200,000) flip the sign of their wall distance
   between float32 and float64. They sit 0.002-0.32 L from the boundary. For every one of them the closest feature
   is an edge or vertex, and the closest cell has a vertex or edge whose pseudo-normal cancels exactly in float64
   (|sum of normals| / sum of weights = 0.0), so roundoff picks the sign. This dominates ISLA (interacting queries), where a few hundred flipped tokens move the whole field.
2. **The in-model token draw.** 5-5,794 of the 200,000 drawn boundary cells change between float32 and float64 (Morton
   order on 21-bit grids, PPS line). This dominates ISLA-DTT on 00045, 00079, 00083 and 00089.
Both are defects of the geometry, but neither is a translation defect, and float64 would only hide the first: it is
ill-posed in exact arithmetic. Confidence: high for the split (geometry against blocks; draw against wall sign). The
fold explanation of the sign comes from a CPU geometry-only rerun on the same cases (§4). Its counts match the GPU run's
on 4 of 5 cases.

## 4. Why the geometry is float32-sensitive (CPU, geometry only; out/geom/geom_33496[2-5].json)

exact_boundary_geometry was run in float32 and in float64 on each case's 200,000 points (the datapipe's draw, on CPU).
On the five geometries with flips, every changed point has the same closest cell in both precisions, and its closest
point agrees to ≤ 5e-8 L. None of them lies on a cell interior: the closest feature is an edge or a vertex. The face
normals agree (none flipped). The feature pseudo-normal differs, however (float32·float64 dot −1.0 to 0.78). For every
changed point, the closest cell has a vertex or edge whose float64 cancellation ratio (|sum of incident unit normals,
angle-weighted at a vertex| / sum of weights) is exactly 0.0. The surface folds back on itself there, for example a
zero-thickness fin, coincident oppositely oriented cells, or roles that meet back to back after the weld. I did not
look at the meshes to tell which. The changed points come from 1-25 distinct cells per case, on no_slip and slip.
The geometries hold 2-1,116 such folded cells. A fold alone does not cause a flip: 00089 has 180 folded cells and no
flip. The flip count from the GPU's asinh RMS matches the CPU's on 00002, 00022, 00045 and 00083 (30/40, 395/427,
74/69, 11/12). On 00019 the GPU implies about 850 flips against the CPU's 40, a difference I have not explained.
Separately, some fluid points carry a negative wall distance in both precisions (00079: 51; 00037: 118; 00002: 83 in
float64). That sign is wrong whatever the precision.

## 5. What it means for the program

- The float32 floor is not a network-conditioning problem, and keeping coordinates in raw metres is not the issue: centring does not
  help. Its causes are two discrete, precision-dependent choices in the geometry: (a) the pseudo-normal sign at
  folds, ill-posed in exact arithmetic (T64 against A64 shows that a one-ulp perturbation of the input moves even the
  float64 output by 1.4e-4 to 1e-1 of RMS(target)); (b) the draw's Morton quantization and PPS line, whose output changes under
  roundoff. Option (a) has principled fixes: a sign that does not divide by a cancelling sum, such as a generalized
  winding number, which is robust to open and folded surfaces; or using the closest cell's own face normal at a fold.
  Float64 pseudo-normals would make the sign reproducible but leave it arbitrary at folds. Option (b): run the Morton
  quantization in float64 (cheap), which makes the draw precision-independent.
- **The draw-to-draw variation is the larger noise source.** Changing only the systematic start (S32) moves a case's
  prediction by 1.8e-2 to 1.5e-1 of RMS(target) on the high-d cases, and by up to 1.1e-1 on a control (DTT 00042;
  ISLA 00037 only 3e-4). ISLA-DTT is the more draw-sensitive model. The scorer's fixed start (EVAL_START = 0.5) picks
  one draw per case, so per-case AeroZoo numbers carry percent-level draw noise whatever the precision. Averages over
  the test set should mostly absorb it; I did not measure that.
- The compiled-path verdict of CMPDIAG stands: the compiled path matches eager to ≤ 6e-6, far inside both floors.

## For the book (draft)

On AeroZoo, ISLA's float32 and float64 predictions differ by up to 28 % of RMS(target) on a few cases, while on AirfRANS
they differ by at most 2e-4. Swapping precision at the boundary between geometry and network puts the whole gap
in the geometry and none in the network (10 of 10 cases). Within the geometry, two discrete steps react to roundoff:
the sign of the wall distance at folded surface features, where the pseudo-normal cancels, and the in-model draw of
boundary tokens. Moving the coordinates to the origin does not help, so the cause is not translation sensitivity.
Changing only the draw's systematic start moves single-case predictions by 2-15 %, which is a larger effect than the
precision gap.
